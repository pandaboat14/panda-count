The War Codex

Every invasion is now a battle you can watch: your squad against theirs, a move picked from a deep menu, and dice that decide how well it lands. Risk still sits underneath, so dice pair off highest against highest, ties go to the defender, and every loss is a real unit gone from the map.

            The procedure

            How a round works

            A battle is a run of rounds. Every round follows the same nine steps, in this order.

            1. Both sides choose at the same time

              A move (Strike, Guard, Tactic or Signature), a Bag item, a switch to another squad, or, for attackers only, a retreat. The defender's choice comes from their Standing Orders, or from them directly if they're online (Live Defence).

              Why

              The defender is usually asleep, so the game can't wait for them. Their Standing Orders fight for them instead.

            2. Support lands first

              Switches, heals, reinforcements, smoke, caltrops and Thunder all happen before anyone throws.

            3. Both sides throw their dice together

              The dice count and bonus come from the move, the unit's attack (attackers) or defence (defenders), hero aura, Forts, statuses, terrain and gear. Nobody rolls more than 4 dice or fewer than 1.

              Why

              Attackers add attack and defenders add defence, exactly as today. Pandas stay stubborn defenders, and ogres stay terrible ones.

              Defenders roll one die fewer from Strikes and Signatures (3 becomes 2, 2 becomes 1). That's Risk's 3 against 2. Without it, defenders got heavy two-dice moves for free.

            4. Reactions

              After seeing the dice, either side may spend a Lucky Gem (re-throw one die), a Chance Cube Blessing (ties go to you) or the Josserkid's Loaded Dice (one die becomes a 6). Each side gets one reaction a round.

            5. Pair off, highest against highest

              The higher total wins the pair. Ties go to the defender, but Guard moves, Blessings and Dazzled can change that. A die with no partner does nothing.

              Why

              Guard moves add no dice bonus. Instead, ties go to you and lost pairs hurt 25% less (Phalanx: 40%, Bamboo Wall: 50%). For attackers, this flips the defender's tie advantage for a round.

            6. Every pair you win hurts

              Each one deals your move's power to the squad on point: ×1.5 if your type beats theirs ("Super effective!"), ×0.75 if theirs beats yours ("Not very effective..."), ×0.75 against Legends, and ×1.5 on a crit (a winning natural 6). Every 10 damage fells one unit, and wounds carry over.

              Why

              HP is 10 for every unit. Different HP per unit double-counted the attack and defence stats the game already has, and made armed pandas beat pandas every single time. With equal HP, a lost pair still costs about one unit, as in Risk, and moves shift it: heavy hits deal 13 to 16, ranged and guard moves 7 or 8.

              Crits land on a natural 6, on 5 or 6 in Gem Caves and for some moves, and on 4 or better for Golden Hour.

            7. Effects fire

              Move effects go off (on a win, on doubles, on a crit, or always). Statuses tick down, and a wiped-out squad is replaced by the next one in line.

            8. Momentum builds

              +1 for every pair won, up to 5. At 5, the squad on point can unleash its Signature move, which spends it all.

            9. The battle ends

              When one side has nobody standing, when the attackers retreat (the defenders get a parting shot of 2 dice, each 5 or better dealing 8, unless the line was cut or smoked), after a Ceasefire, or after 20 rounds, when the invasion stalls and the attackers go home.

            The matchups

            Types

            Every squad has a type. Four of them form a ring: each beats the next one round, and opposites are even. Heroes are Legends and stand outside the ring.

              Who fights

              Units and heroes

              Attack and defence are the numbers you already know: they add to every die. HP is new, and it's the same 10 for every unit.

              Heroes

              Heroes join a battle fought from or in their region. Their aura adds to every die your side rolls, as it does today, and they also take the field as a one-hero squad you can switch in.

              The menu

              Moves

              Every squad brings its own moves, and gear adds more. Strikes deal damage. Guards win ties and soften blows. Tactics mostly deal nothing and do something else. Signatures need full Momentum.

              Dice counts are written for attackers. Defending, a Strike or Signature rolls one die fewer (3 becomes 2, 2 becomes 1, never below 1). Guards and Tactics roll as written. That's Risk's 3 against 2.

              Spending your resources

              The Armory and the Bag

              Resources get new jobs. The Armory turns them into gear a squad carries into battle. The Bag holds power-ups you spend mid-fight.

              Weapons and armour

              Each squad carries one weapon and one piece of armour, bought at the Bank's Armory for a region's stack. Gear breaks after 3 battles.

              The Bag

              Power-ups bought at the Bank with resources. When you can use one depends on the item.

              Statuses

              A status is set by one round and felt in the next. Most last a single round.

                Terrain

                Terrain comes from the region's resource. Each one gives moves of its element +1 die and adds a rule of its own.

                  Buildings in battle

                  A defender's buildings join the fight.

                    World events in battle

                    Events that already exist in the game reach into battles too.

                      Defending while you sleep

                      Standing Orders and Live Defence

                      The game is asynchronous, so the defender is usually away. You set Standing Orders for each region on your own turn, and they fight for you until you're back.

                      The native nations

                      Natives can't change their minds. Each nation always fights the same way.

                        Live Defence

                        If you're online when someone invades, you get a prompt to take command. You then choose each round yourself, with a 20-second clock. Whenever the clock runs out, your orders take over for that round.

                        Computer players use the same battle AI, flavoured by their own doctrine.

                        After the fact

                        Replays and catching up

                        Every battle keeps a full record: the starting armies, each round's two choices, every die, every re-throw, and the seed of each throw.

                        The server does the rolling. Your browser then throws real-physics dice that are simulated in advance to land on the server's numbers, so the same seed replays the identical tumble for everyone.

                        When you come back, "Since your last turn" lists every battle you're allowed to see (fog rules apply) as a card. Tap one for the full replay at 1x, 2x or 4x, with a round scrubber marked for crits, knockouts and super-effective hits. Or watch a 20-second highlight reel.

                        Defenders see what their Standing Orders chose, and why.

                        For the build

                        How it plugs into the code

                        The rules on this page are a working engine already: shared/codex-data.js holds every number and word (CODEX), and shared/battle-engine.js is pure rules over a plain object, with no rendering (Battle).

                        • src/game/engine.tsA move into an enemy region opens s.battle instead of resolving it on the spot. New actions: battleRound { action, prep? }, battleReact { id, die } and battleAuto. The server rolls with the game's existing seeded RNG. Ending your turn with a battle open lets Sun Tzu finish it.
                        • The battle eventIt carries BattleData v2, the full round log. Old battles still replay in today's BattleView.
                        • Standing OrdersThey live on each owned region: orders: { doctrine, lead, traps, budget }. Computer players use the same battle AI with their doctrine.
                        • The BankIt gets an Armory tab (gear per region stack, breaks after 3 battles) and a Bag (items per player).
                        • OddsThe previews in odds.ts become Battle.preview. This page's Dice Lab uses Battle.estimate, the same machinery with more throws.
                        • The screenA new R3F BattleScreen replaces BattleView, and its dice tray component is shared with the start-of-turn roll.
                        • This pageThe clash calls Battle.createBattle, beginRound, react and finishRound, and prints the event texts. Mei's orders come from Battle.aiAction.

                        Every number, move and line of flavour on this page is read from the battle engine's data, and the clash and the Dice Lab run the engine itself. Balance figures come from the simulation run for this draft.
